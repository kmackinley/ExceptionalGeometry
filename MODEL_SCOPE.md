# Mathematical Model and Physical Scope

**Last reviewed:** 17 August 2026

Exceptional Geometry uses several exact mathematical structures associated with \(E_8\), including its root lattice, Coxeter element, modular forms, spectral functions, and representation theory. These structures can be studied independently of any physical interpretation.

## Public interpretation rule

The Coxeter element and the torus built from the \(E_8\) lattice are used as **mathematical tools** for finite-order symmetry, fixed-point, lattice, and spectral calculations. This repository does **not** claim that ordinary physical space, spacetime, or the universe is literally a Coxeter torus.

When a manuscript uses a torus or quotient as an internal compactification, that is one candidate effective model. Connecting its mathematical data to generations, masses, couplings, gravity, or cosmology requires additional physical assumptions: a consistent action, field content, boundary conditions, bundle data, normalized operators, and a valid dimensional reduction.

Accordingly:

- exact lattice, determinant, and matrix identities are reported as mathematical results;
- mappings from those identities to observed particles are reported as conditional models or phenomenology;
- numerical agreement is not treated as evidence that a specific internal geometry is physically real;
- the matrix-brane results form a related but distinct research track and do not prove the compactification model.

## Current model variants

### Variant A - Einstein-Yang-Mills starting point

The synthesis manuscript uses a twelve-dimensional Einstein-Hilbert term together with an \(E_8\) Yang-Mills field and adjoint fermions.

### Variant B - Pure Yang-Mills emergent gravity

The Newton manuscript treats pure twelve-dimensional \(E_8\) Yang-Mills as the starting action and attempts to recover the gravitational frame field from mixed-index gauge components.

These variants are not yet one theory. A future model-definition paper must choose or reconcile them and state the exact internal space, real form, representations, independent dimensional scales, and boundary conditions.
