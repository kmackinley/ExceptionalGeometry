# Prediction and Observation Log

**Frozen-status review date:** 17 August 2026

This log distinguishes three questions:

1. Was the numerical value written down before the later result?
2. Was the observable already approximately known or used during construction?
3. Does the later measurement uniquely select the framework over nearby alternatives?

A close numerical match is recorded as agreement. It is called confirmation only if the test is prospective, discriminating, and difficult to reproduce without the mechanism.

## Interpretation notes

### Higgs mass

This is the strongest new numerical development. The latest 13 TeV-only measurement is close, and the CMS diphoton combination has the same rounded central value. It is not a blind test because the Higgs mass was already measured near 125 GeV before the formula was proposed, and the quartic relation itself remains a conjectural Hosotani result.

### Neutrino mass sum

Do not retune the value. The fact that 61 meV lies at the normal-ordering floor makes it useful precisely because current cosmological combinations are beginning to probe it. All public statements must identify the cosmological model and data combination attached to an upper limit.

### No axion

A discovery of a genuine QCD/Peccei–Quinn axion would contradict the proposed strong-CP resolution. Discovery of an unrelated axion-like particle would not automatically do so.
